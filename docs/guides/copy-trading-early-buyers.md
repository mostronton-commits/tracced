# Can you copy trade the wallets that bought first?

Every list of "wallets to copy" is built the same way: the wallets that made the most. The question it skips is whether
you could have made it too, buying a few seconds after them. Here is what 159 analysed tokens say.

## They are fast

Of the early buyers who sold out, half held less than **7 minutes** and a quarter less than **a minute and a half**. A copy
that arrives 20 seconds late on a 90-second trade buys a different price and often sells after the top.

## Most of them do not make much

57% of early buyers closed in profit, but the median realized profit was **$5.79** on a median position of **$175**. Only
**13.5%** sold at twice their entry or better, and **1.1%** at ten times. Copying a random early buyer is close to copying
the market.

## A few make most of it

In a typical token the best tenth of the early wallets took **67%** of the profit. That is the case for copying: not
everyone, but the few wallets that are early and right again and again. One good pump proves little; ten do.

## How to pick wallets worth following

1. **Look across tokens, not one.** On tracced, open a wallet's card: its last 30 days on every token, the win rate, the
   PnL, how long it holds.
2. **Prefer holders to flippers.** A wallet that holds for hours can be followed by a person. One that holds 40 seconds
   can be followed only by a bot, and the bots are already there.
3. **Leave out what you cannot copy.** Snipers, bot-like wallets and wallets inside the creator's bundle bought where you
   never could. tracced tags them, and its monthly
   [top traders](/lists/top-traders) list leaves them out.
4. **Check the numbers.** A profit figure that comes from tokens transferred in, not bought, is not a trade. Every row on
   tracced opens on Solscan.

## Follow instead of copy

Saved wallets on tracced can send a Telegram message within seconds of a buy or a sell. You see what they did and decide
yourself, at your own size. That is slower than a copy bot and safer than one.
